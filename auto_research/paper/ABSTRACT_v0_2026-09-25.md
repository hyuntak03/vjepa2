## Abstract (v0)

In the physical world, an intelligent system anticipates the near future by evolving the state it has observed: even when observation is incomplete—an object slips behind an occluder, a hand leaves the frame—the anticipated future keeps moving. Joint-embedding predictive architectures (JEPA) are the leading candidates for such latent world models, and two lines of evidence are taken to show that they learn the physical world: intuitive-physics benchmarks and state-of-the-art action anticipation, both computed from the pretrained encoder–predictor pair. We ask what the predictor actually carries forward once observation stops. Using a scene-level, label-free readout calibrated on synthetic ground truth and validated on real video, we find that the pretrained predictor—and predictors re-trained purely for future prediction—do not evolve a state; they fill the future by retrieving from the last observation. Content is transported only a bounded distance from where it was last seen (2–5 cells; about half of the moving content in real video lies beyond it), content that is occluded at the last observation is not carried at all, and the model's own predictions cannot serve as new observations. The information is nonetheless present—probes read it and a single attention intervention places it correctly—so what is missing is not representation but a carried state. The benchmarks behind the world-model reading do not probe this regime: copying the last latent matches VoE accuracy, and replacing the predictor's output barely changes anticipation. We therefore (i) propose evaluation protocols that measure state evolution directly—reach and permanence curves on synthetic and real video—and (ii) ★ introduce a latent world model that maintains an explicit state, rolled forward without observation and read by the pretrained predictor through a lightweight adapter, trained with multi-step video prediction. ★ It extends reach and permanence on real video without hurting existing benchmarks, and improves long-horizon anticipation on EK100 and Ego4D/EgoExo4D and occlusion cases on IntPhys 2. Action conditioning, as in planning, then builds on a state that already evolves on its own.
