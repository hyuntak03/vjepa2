## 1. Meta-review

**Recommendation: weak reject (3/6) in the current form. Confidence 4/5.**
- All five reviewers settled at 3 after discussion.
- **Expected range after revision:**
  - **4 (poster)** if the writing fixes, the CPU re-analyses and experiments B1, B2 and B5 below are done.
  - **5** only if the paper also adds a documented within-run dissociation, a mechanism for the observation-fed step, and the full-grid VoE audit.
- **Oral: no.** The reasons are a single frozen model, a qualitative predictor result that readers will expect, and page-1 mechanism claims that change with the readout. The authors should not attempt multi-model replication; the constraints exclude it, and the score does not depend on it.

### What the paper does
It keeps the released V-JEPA 2 ViT-H encoder and EMA target frozen and swaps only the predictor: the released one plus three existing checkpoints. It asks whether the predictor advances a latent state once observations stop. The tools are:
- parameter-free template readouts;
- other-trajectory nulls;
- copy, constant-velocity and freeze-K oracles;
- closure tests;
- patching and knockout interventions;
- data from RollOut_v3, IntPhysGen v11, IntPhys 1 and 677 SSv2 clips.

### What the panel values
1. **The VoE audit.** This is new for this protocol.
   - Copying the last latent scores 85.00 against 88.89 at the best IntPhys 1 cell.
   - Pre-event tubelets that are pixel-identical are discriminated at 90.4% with the bidirectional target and at exactly 50.0% with a causal target.
   - On v11, the released predictor's +2.5 margin over copy comes entirely from colour violations. Without colour it is −1.7 [−2.6, −0.7].
   - On SSv2, blurred copies reproduce 61–79% of the predictor's advantage over copy.
2. **The constructive swap.**
   - When fed real observations, all four predictors take the same one-step transition (`ar_z` 0.355–0.388).
   - They differ only open-loop. At s1, t10 hit−null is −0.12 for the released predictor and +0.23 / +0.24 for pv1 / Ariel.
   - Ariel was trained only on SSv2 and K400, and here it is queried for 16 slots against the ≤ 8 it was trained on. It still carries the object.
   - This is the cleanest localization in the paper, and the draft buries it.
3. **Measurement discipline.** Nulls, oracles through the same readout, trajectory-cluster CIs, and retractions kept on the record.
4. **The closure design rules out the adapter.** `ar_hcA − ar_z` = −0.000 [−0.006, +0.005]. The format and content effects are separated: −0.148 and −0.224.

### What keeps it below the bar (ranked)
1. **The rollout modes are conflated.** A co-author read the draft as "predict one tubelet, chain it, it works", and every evidence doc now needs a §0 box to say otherwise. On page 1, finding (a), finding (b), the §5.3 list and the §7 "self-feedback slope" row all invite this reading.
2. **Numbers in the draft are stale, misquoted or retracted in the evidence.** Each is listed in §4 below, among them:
   - §5.1: "R² 0.99" and "rejects a fixed tubelet budget";
   - §6.1: 0.94 / 0.27 / 0.36 and "timing twins";
   - §7: 80.83, and "extrapolator 0.74" reported as a raw hit;
   - §3: "84 × 28";
   - §4.1: pooled R² given as the headline.
3. **Page-1 mechanism claims change with the readout, and no primary readout is declared.** Under the appearance template:
   - `tf_first − ar_z` = −0.067 [−0.106, −0.032] and `tf_last − ar_z` = −0.071 [−0.102, −0.036], so finding (b) disappears;
   - `ar_hcW − ar_z` = −0.014 [−0.043, +0.016];
   - "chain worse than one-shot" survives only as new-cell landing at j3–4 (+0.237 / +0.377). The slope difference is +0.007 [−0.025, +0.044].
4. **Encoder sufficiency is overclaimed, and the claim had already been retracted.**
   - M13 correction 2 withdrew "encoder is sufficient". The draft brings it back as the §4.3 title "Sufficiency", the §7 title "The encoder is enough" and abstract (iii).
   - The extrapolator uses the **true** last pixel position.
   - A label-mean velocity already gives 0.56–0.61.
   - On arc the predictor beats the extrapolator (t6 .85 vs .50; t8 .55 vs .00).
   - Acceleration is neither readable nor transferred, and occluded state is untested.
5. **§8 "non-monotone" rests on n = 4 confounded predictors, and data the authors already hold contradict it.**
   - Across its own run, Ariel's IntPhys score rises: ep5 → ep43 is +21.7 at skip2_w32 and +21.1 [+15.6, +27.2] at skip2_w16.
   - On v11, every trained predictor beats the released one (75.8 → 79.4–91.4).
6. **First-pass results appear in the abstract, and the [V] tag means "numbers reproduced", not "claim confirmed".**
   - VERIFY rates as suggestive: §4.3, §5.1 integration gain, §5.2 SSv2 diagonal, and the M2 position rows.
   - The abstract's parts (ii) "last token alone" and "pulled back", all of (iii), and (iv) "non-monotone" are first-pass (tagged [1st]).
7. **The timescale is not disclosed.**
   - Pretraining runs at 4 fps (0.5 s per tubelet; `configs/train/vith16/cooldown-256px-64f.yaml:23`). v3 at stride 1 is 30 fps (67 ms per tubelet).
   - §7 is reported at s1 only, although `cmp_predictors.json` also holds s2 and s4.
8. **The central predictor finding is the expected result.** A tube-mask infiller is a poor open-loop forecaster, and a forecasting-trained head does better. V-JEPA 2-AC, DINO-world, DINO-WM and HERA are consistent with this. Contributions 2–3 are publishable only as support for the audit and the dissociation, not as the headline.

- **D2 (CCA as headline).** No. CCA is a restatement of the McNemar table; it needs the reverse conditional and the visible control beside it.

  - IntPhys 1: 44 vs 25 (w32), 53 vs 17 (w16), 19 vs 27 (skip5_w16).

- **D3 (stride).** I re-derived these from `cmp_predictors.json`.
  - **s2 holds:**

    |---|---|---|---|
    | t4 hit−null | +0.245 | +0.331 | +0.311 |
    | t7 hit−null (≈1 s) | −0.092 | +0.250 | +0.197 |

## 0. 질문에 대한 답

**아니다.** "미래를 튜블릿 1개씩만 예측하게 해서 이어붙였더니 된다"는 결론은 초안 어디에도 없다. 오히려 그 방식이 **가장 분명하게 실패했다.** 모델을 1튜블릿 예측용으로 다시 학습한 적도 없다. 릴리즈 predictor에 매 걸음 미래 1튜블릿만 질의한 것뿐이다.

- 셋째 줄이 되먹인 관측을 그대로 베낀 것인지도 확인했다. 두 템플릿 모두에서 새 칸 착지가 이전 칸 착지보다 높다.
  - 진실 칸 템플릿: .81–.95 vs ≤ .09
  - 외형 템플릿: .39–.48 vs .07–.19
  - 출처: `/data/hyuntak/project/2026/2027_cvpr/vjepa2/auto_research/exp_results/p3/p3_v3_p3b.json`
- 학습된 predictor도 자기 출력을 이어붙이면 0.14 / 0.23 / 0.15에 그친다. 진실의 1/3–1/2이다.

**그래서 논문의 주장은 이렇다.**
- 릴리즈 predictor는 관측이 끊기면 몇 걸음만 가고 멈춘다. 자기 출력을 이어붙이면 더 일찍 멈춘다.
- 같은 encoder 위에 미래 예측으로 학습된 predictor를 끼우면 한 번에 훨씬 멀리 간다.
- 그러니 병목은 encoder가 아니라 predictor다.

오독이 생기는 곳은 초안 §1 (a)와 §5.3의 문장이다. 다섯 리뷰어가 모두 이것을 약점으로 꼽았다.

## Review: CVPR Area Chair perspective

**Paper:** "Seeing Is Not Evolving…" (`/data/hyuntak/project/2026/2027_cvpr/vjepa2/auto_research/paper/DRAFT_v1_2026-09-25.md`)

### Summary

The paper studies the released V-JEPA 2 ViT-H, keeping the encoder frozen and swapping predictors. It uses parameter-free template readouts on synthetic motion (RollOut_v3), IntPhysGen v11 and SSv2 left/right clips. It claims four things:
1. The encoder exposes velocity and direction, and it summarizes history into the boundary tokens.
2. The pretrained predictor extrapolates a few steps, is anchored to the last token, is pulled back by history tokens, and stalls under self-feedback.
3. Predictors trained for future prediction on the same encoder carry objects far, so the encoder is not the bottleneck.
4. VoE surprise does not track this ability.

### Strengths

1. **The measurement discipline is unusually strong** for an analysis paper: parameter-free readouts, same-law other-trajectory nulls, copy / constant-velocity / freeze-K oracles, trajectory-cluster CIs, and an adversarial verification pass whose corrections appear in the text.
2. **The closure test is the most non-obvious result.** The pretrained predictor was never trained on future-only queries, yet it contains a one-step transition: real causal observations give 0.361, 82% of truth. The linear adapter is ruled out (ar_hcA − ar_z = −0.000 [−0.006, +0.005]).
3. **The VoE audit components are genuinely new for this protocol:**
   - Copy of the last latent scores 85.00 on IntPhys 1.
   - The bidirectional target lets pixel-identical pre-event tubelets be discriminated at 90.4%.
   - On v11, the released predictor's +2.5 margin over copy comes entirely from colour violations.
4. **The constructive comparison with existing checkpoints is a sensible, constraint-respecting way to localize the bottleneck.** Ariel is trained only on natural video and still carries the object on unseen synthetic scenes (appearance hit 0.73 vs encoder ceiling 0.80).

### Weaknesses (ranked, each with the minimal fix)

**W1. "Known result" risk; significance rests on the wrong pillar.**
- The released predictor was trained to fill tube masks spanning the whole clip. That it is a poor multi-step forecaster, and that a forecasting-trained head on the same encoder does better, is the expected outcome.
- Meta retrains the predictor for V-JEPA 2-AC (`load_predictor: false`). DINO-world, DINO-WM and JEPA-WMs already establish "frozen encoder + future predictor". Garrido reports a 3–4 s memory limit, and HERA is motivated by the same failure.
- Contributions 2–3 will read as "an infiller is not a simulator".
- The genuinely new, field-relevant result is §8: VoE scores are largely change detection plus target leakage. Right now §8 is the thinnest section, and the copy-conditional accuracy (CCA) is missing. CCA is already computed in `REVIEW_CVPR_ORAL_2026-09-25.md` §4: on v11, when copy is wrong, the predictor is at 51.8 [49.2, 54.2], i.e. chance. The visible-condition control is 72.4. IntPhys 1 is 44% at the best cell and 53% at skip2_w16.
- **Minimal fix:** make the one-sentence message the dissociation. For example: *"The predictor that VoE evaluations credit with intuitive physics mostly detects change from the last observation. It advances visible objects for only ~3–5 tubelets and cannot continue from its own outputs, while the same frozen encoder supports far longer extrapolation under forecasting-trained predictors."* Move CCA, the full-grid copy table and leakage to §4, and move the mechanism to §5–7 as the explanation.

**W2. The one-sentence message is unclear, and the rollout modes are conflated.**
- The draft has 4 contributions, 3 "non-obvious findings" and 5 requirements.
- §5.3 lists paired Δ-slopes vs ar_z as bare numbers ("+0.000", "−0.373"). "+0.000" reads as a stalled slope when it means "same as real observations".
- §1(a) "fresh observations advance the object every step" and §7 "self-feedback slope" invite exactly the misreading above. Every evidence doc (P2, P3, M2, M13) now carries a §0 box answering "no, we did not concatenate one-tubelet predictions", so this is a recurring misreading.
- **Fix:** add a page-1 figure/table of the four modes, as above, with an explicit "uses future frames" column. Report absolute slopes with truth, and put Δs in a separate column.

**W3. Headline finding (b), "the step is read from the last token alone", depends on the template.**
- The draft's own §3 rule says truth-cell templates are always paired with an appearance template.
- Under the appearance template (P3B §3), tf_first = −0.067 and tf_last = −0.071. They are indistinguishable, so the last-token vs history asymmetry disappears.
- Only "a chain of self-predictions stalls" (−0.314 appearance) survives both readouts. The finding is also [1st], unverified.
- **Fix:** demote (b) from page 1, report both templates, and state the robust version: single-token replacement anywhere costs ~0.07, while chaining costs ~0.31–0.37.

**W4. "The encoder is enough / not the bottleneck" is overclaimed.**
- The 0.74 "encoder-velocity extrapolator" uses the **ground-truth last pixel position** plus ridge-read velocity (M13 correction 2).
  - A label-mean velocity already gives 0.61, so the z-specific gain is +0.14.
  - On arc, the predictor beats it (t6 .85 vs .50; t8 .55 vs .00).
  - It is an oracle, not a ceiling, so "up to the encoder-state extrapolator" and "matching" imply a bound that is not established.
- The appearance "encoder ceiling 0.80" is the bidirectional target encoder applied to the true future.
- What is supported: for visible, near-constant-velocity objects, the frozen encoder's tokens support ~t10 placement when the predictor is trained for it.
  - Acceleration is not readable at short context (H1) and is not transferred by any predictor.
  - Occluded state is untested.
  - Presence fades for Ariel: objectness 0.49 at t10 and 0.25 at t15, and the parallel attention-ruler analysis finds "opposite by definition".
- Even trained predictors reach ≤0.23 of 0.44 under self-feedback, so the input/output space mismatch remains a candidate bottleneck.
- **Fix:** scope the title and abstract (iii) to "visible, near-constant-velocity motion". Rename the extrapolator a "GT-position + z-velocity oracle". Report hit − null rather than raw hits: the nulls differ from 0.20 (released) to 0.50 (Ariel).

**W5. The §8 "non-monotone" claim is confounded and selectively reported.**
- Ariel's IntPhys 1 score of 69.44 is obtained outside its training regime:
  - it was trained with ≤8 predicted slots at 12 fps on 48-frame windows, but skip2_w32 queries 14 slots with C=4;
  - it is **below copy** by −16.1 [−23.3, −8.9] (TrainingEffects §1-1).
- On v11, Ariel beats the released predictor (79.4 vs 75.8), and v11_postft is worst on v3 but best on v11 VoE (91.4).
- DINO-world, a forecasting-trained predictor, scores 91.3 on IntPhys.
- With 4 predictors, 1 seed and ±5–7 pt CIs, "the best evolver scores lowest" is an anecdote.
- The v11_postft IntPhys entry of 80.83 is the withdrawn **Max** metric. The AvgSurprise number is 77.22 (TRAINED_PREDICTORS §3; CLAUDE.md §2-7). The row mixes metrics.
- **Fix:** replace this with "VoE accuracy and kinematic evolution are dissociable". Report both benchmarks (IntPhys 1 and v11), use AvgSurprise throughout, and add Ariel in its native window regime.

**W6. The history story is internally inconsistent.** The draft makes five history claims from different manipulations and readouts, with no reconciling sentence:
- §1(b)/§5.3: the step comes from the last token; history is harmless.
- §6.3: history tokens pull predictions backward.
- §7: the predictor is insensitive to a different-trajectory history swap.
- §6.1: static objects are maintained from history.
- §4.2: the encoder summarizes history into the boundary tokens.

§9 requirement 1 ("carried state not a function of the last token alone") is then built on the weakest of them.

Additional problems:
- M6's shifts are measured against base_iso, which itself differs from base by −0.56 at t4.
- At t4 the dose-response is not monotone (+0.02 / −0.18 / +0.39).
- The bndM arm feeds the encoder only 4 frames, which is out of distribution for the encoder, so "summarization" is confounded with degradation on short input.

**Fix:** state one sentence and put the arms in one table: "same-clip history tokens are retrieved as past object positions (pull back), while scene-discontinuous history is ignored." Add the bndM control in E3 below.

**W7. Misquoted or retracted items remain in v1.**

# Review: "Seeing Is Not Evolving" (DRAFT v1, 2026-09-25)

**Reviewer perspective:** a co-author of JEPA / V-JEPA-style world models.

## 1. Summary

The paper studies the frozen V-JEPA 2 ViT-H: the online context encoder, the EMA target encoder, and the released predictor. It asks whether the predictor advances a latent state once observations stop. The tools are:
- synthetic motion (RollOut_v3), v11 occlusion-timing twins, IntPhys 1, and SSv2 left/right;
- parameter-free template readouts, trajectory-cluster CIs, copy / constant-velocity / freeze-K baselines;
- closure tests (feeding real observations vs the model's own predictions back in), history and boundary patching, and velocity steering.

The paper makes four claims:
- (i) The encoder exposes position and velocity at the predictor interface.
- (ii) The released predictor extrapolates only a few steps, lags, and fades. Fed its own outputs, it stalls.
- (iii) Predictors already trained for future prediction on the same frozen encoder carry the object much farther, so the encoder is not the bottleneck.
- (iv) VoE surprise does not track this ability: a copy-last-latent baseline nearly matches the released predictor on IntPhys 1, and bidirectional targets leak.

It ends with a signature-based test suite and design requirements.

## 2. Strengths

- **The measurement discipline is unusually good for this genre.**
  - CIs are trajectory-cluster bootstraps (84 trajectories × 28 appearances).
  - Nulls are computed over all other trajectories of the same law.
  - Oracle predictors are scored through the same readout (P2 §3-4).
  - Truth-cell and appearance templates are reported side by side.
  - Retractions are kept visible.
- **The VoE audit is the most novel and most useful part** (§8, H6).
  - Copy-last-latent reaches 85.00 against 88.89 in the best Garrido cell.
  - Pre-event tubelets, where pixels and p are identical, are discriminated at 90.4% with the standard bidirectional target and exactly 50.0% with a causal target.
  - Blurred copies reproduce 61–79% of the predictor's L1 advantage over copy on SSv2.
  - Our own IntPhys/VoE numbers depend on the bidirectional target and on the grid, so this matters to us directly.
- **The constructive control is the right design** (§7). Same frozen encoder, same EMA targets, same readouts; only the predictor changes. Ariel is trained only on SSv2+K400 and still reaches 0.73 on v3 (released 0.04; the encoder's own ceiling on true future frames, same template, is 0.80). That is strong evidence the encoder supports near-constant-velocity carrying.
- **The paper already frames the pretrained predictor's failures as an evaluation-validity question rather than a capability verdict** (§1, §8), and it makes no planning claim.

## 3. Weaknesses (ranked, each with the minimal fix)

**W1. The frame-rate and training-regime mismatch is never disclosed, and only partly tested.**
- V-JEPA 2 pretraining/cooldown runs at `fps: 4` (`configs/train/vith16/cooldown-256px-64f.yaml:23`), so one tubelet is 0.5 s.
- RollOut_v3 at stride 1 is 30 fps, so one tubelet is 67 ms: **7.5× faster**. The largest stride tested (s4, 7.5 fps) is still 2× faster.
- On top of this, the predictor was trained with tube masks spanning the whole clip (`temporal_scale [1,1]`). Future-only queries are off-label on two axes at once.
- In the model's native units, the stride-1 "~5 tubelets before lagging" (P2 §3-5) is about 2.5 s. That is not an obviously short horizon for a deterministic predictor trained on 4 fps natural video. DINO-world, trained future-only, reports failure near 1 s.
- The title and abstract ("only extrapolates a few steps") read as a capability statement about V-JEPA 2.
- **Minimal fix:**
  - State 4 fps in §3.
  - Report every horizon in seconds and in native (0.5 s) tubelets.
  - Add a near-native-rate check using existing data (E1).
  - Retitle to "the tube-mask-pretrained predictor".

**W2. The central mechanistic claims depend on the readout, and the teacher-forced "one-step transition" has no copy control.**
- **Page-1 finding (b)** ("history of own predictions is harmless if the last token is real"):
  - Truth-cell template: `tf_first` +0.023, `tf_last` −0.102.
  - Appearance template, which uses no future truth: `tf_first` −0.067 [−0.106, −0.032], `tf_last` −0.071 [−0.102, −0.036]. These are indistinguishable.
  - Under the leakage-free readout, (b) and design-requirement row 1 are not supported. The robust statement is that one self-predicted token costs about 0.07 wherever it sits, and the full chain costs 0.31–0.37.
- **Finding (a)** ("fresh observations advance the object every step"):
  - In teacher-forced arms, the slope cannot separate "advance one step" from "copy the fed observation". Copying T_{j−1} also gives slope 0.44.
  - The statistic that does separate them, new-cell landing, is .81–.95 with the truth template but .39–.48 with the appearance template. The appearance-template rate of landing on the previous cell T_{j−1} is not reported.
- **Minimal fix:**
  - Add a copy-the-fed-observation oracle through the same readout.
  - Report previous-cell landing under both templates.
  - Add a sub-cell (soft-argmax) displacement relative to the fed position. Per-step motion at stride 1 is 0.44 of an 18-px cell, while the hit tolerance is ±1 cell.
  - Demote (b) from page 1 unless it survives these checks.

**W3. Several stated numbers disagree with the evidence files. Fix all of these before any external reading.**
- **§5.1, "a freeze-after-3-steps oracle fits it (R² 0.99)".**
  - 0.991 is the fit of the D0+kδ model to *the oracle's own* cliffs. The predictor's fit is 0.944, and it is not distinguishable from a stride-only model (ΔR² [−0.05, +0.30]).
  - p and K=3 differ: D0 2.62 vs 1.51, k 1.96 vs 2.70, s1_C32 cliffs 7.69/7.28/6.40 vs 5.60/5.37/4.57.
  - "Rejects a fixed tubelet budget" is also wrong. P2 rejects a fixed *cliff tubelet*, not a fixed *step budget*; freeze-K *is* a step budget. The number of tubelets followed is 5 → 3–5 → 3 across strides.
- **§7, IntPhys 1 for v11_postft = 80.83.** This is the withdrawn Max variant. The harness AvgSurprise value is 77.22 [71.1, 83.3] (`z_research/TrainingEffects/scores/SCORES.md`).
- **§6.1, "Position recovers much less (0.27)".** 0.27 includes static objects. For moving objects, which the sentence is about, it is 0.15 [0.09, 0.21]; for constant velocity it is 0.03 [−0.05, 0.10].
  - Recovery 0.94 is threshold-based; the continuous measure gives 1.02 in VERIFY_0925 and 1.10 in the M2 doc.
  - Static destruction ranges 0.36–0.71 depending on threshold.
- **§4.1.** The pooled R² 0.985/0.976 is mostly motion-law identity; a law one-hot alone gives 0.925/0.877. Lead with the within-group numbers: |v| 0.949–0.955, vx 0.80.
- **§4.3.** The constant-velocity extrapolator's 0.74 is a raw hit rate built on the *true* last pixel position; it is compared with p's hit rate and p's null.
  - Use hit − null for both: +0.24 [0.17, 0.30] vs −0.14 [−0.18, −0.09].
  - Report the arc reversal: at t6 the extrapolator gets .50 vs p .85; at t8 .00 vs .55.
- **Abstract (iv) and §8, "copy nearly matches".** This holds in one cell only.
  - In skip2_w16 (the official-config cell) p − copy is +12.2 [+6.7, +17.8].
  - With the causal target in skip2_w32 it is +7.2 [+1.1, +13.9].
  - Copy itself gains +7.8 [+3.3, +13.3] from the bidirectional target. "Copy nearly matches" is therefore a statement about the target as much as about the predictor.

**W4. The VoE non-monotonicity claim (§8 bullet 1) is thin and specific to one benchmark.**
- It rests on four predictors, one seed each, with IntPhys 1 CIs of about ±6.
- On v11_split_test *every* trained predictor improves VoE over release (75.8 → 79.4–91.4), so on the authors' own benchmark the direction partly tracks evolution.
- Ariel is below copy on IntPhys 1 (−16.1 [−23.3, −8.9]). That may reflect its trained horizon (≤ 8 slots) and target regime rather than VoE validity. Ariel is also 63.3 in the w16 cell, so horizon alone does not explain it.
- **Minimal fix:** state it as an existence proof ("a predictor can gain evolution and lose IntPhys score"). Report IntPhys 1 and v11 side by side, and give per-cell {p, copy} × {standard, causal} for all four predictors.

**W5. The trained-predictor comparison (§7) is not apples-to-apples as tabulated.**
- **Stride 1 only.** Ariel was trained at 12 fps (0.167 s tubelets), which is close to v3 stride 2 (0.133 s). At s2, Ariel's cliff slot is 4.9 vs release 4.1; at s1 it is 11.8 vs 6.6. The s1 table flatters the gap.
- **pv1 is near-domain.** It was trained on the same 10° wedge and constant-velocity arm as v3 (TrainingEffects §2). Disclose this; Ariel is the only clean out-of-domain case.
- **v11_postft is evaluated at 3× its training stride.** Its "overshoot" (progress 1.92 at t4) is a unit mismatch. It does not show that "training alone is not sufficient".
- **"Matches the extrapolator (0.74)" compares a template readout with a rule built on the true position.** Use the appearance template with the encoder ceiling (0.80) as the ceiling.
- **Minimal fix:** a full stride table, each predictor also at its training rate, and drop or re-run the v11_postft sentence.

**W6. The objective (hedging) alternative is left to §10, although the authors hold the best counter-argument.**
- Ariel and pv1 are trained with the *same* L1 loss against the *same* frozen EMA targets, yet they carry the object. So deterministic-L1 hedging alone does not explain the fading. The training task (tube masks) and the frame rate are the live candidates. Say this in the main text.
- Two further points for M6:
  - The history-shift experiment gives a discriminating sign test. If the predictor estimated velocity from history displacement, shifting history 8 frames into the past would move predictions *forward*. The observed shift is backward (−0.33), which fits anchoring. Make this explicit.
  - Caveat it: base_iso already differs from base by −0.56 at t4, so every effect is measured against an out-of-distribution baseline.

**W7. Novelty and positioning.**
- Already established:
  - the concept (StEvo-Bench, WRBench);
  - encoder physics probing (Joseph 2026, which also reports acceleration readable and conflicts with H1 — this is unreconciled);
  - frozen encoder plus a trained predictor (V-JEPA 2-AC, DINO-world, DINO-WM).
- Contribution 3 is therefore an expected result. It is valuable as a control, not as a contribution.
- What is new: the VoE audit, the closure test, and component-level localization. Lead with these.
- §1 motivates with IntPhys 2, but IntPhys 2 is never evaluated. Evaluate it or drop the hook.
- I could not independently re-check the 2026 citations (the web-search budget was exhausted). I relied on `auto_research/Archive/LITERATURE_POSITIONING_2026-09-25.md`.

**W8. Much of the new content is unverified.**
- The following are all tagged [1st]:

**W9. The paper never names the use case where the horizon limit bites.**
- In closed-loop use, observations are re-encoded every step; that is the `ar_z` regime, and it works.

**Answer to the question you relayed.** No. The draft does not claim that forcing the model to predict one future tubelet at a time and stitching the outputs together works. The evidence files say so directly (P3 §0, P2 §0, M13 §0, M2 §0):

## Summary
The draft asks whether V-JEPA 2's pretrained predictor evolves the latent state beyond the last observation, using the frozen ViT-H encoder. The main data are controlled synthetic motion (RollOut_v3, v11) and SSv2 left/right clips. Readouts are parameter-free template-argmax with nulls. Its claims:
1. The encoder exposes velocity and summarises history into the boundary tokens.
2. The released predictor extrapolates a few steps from the last token, is pulled back toward past observed positions, fades, and stalls on its own outputs.
3. Predictors trained for future prediction on the same encoder carry the object as far as an extrapolator using velocity read from the encoder, so the encoder is not the bottleneck.
4. VoE surprise does not track this ability.

Several interventions support these claims: boundary patching, history time-shift, attention knockout, velocity steering, and teacher-forced closure arms.

## Strengths
- **Self-correction and statistics.** The corrections are unusually thorough: nulls against every other trajectory of the same law, trajectory-cluster bootstraps, retractions kept on record. Headline numbers were reproduced by independent code (VERIFY_0925).
- **Well-designed closure test (P3/P3b).** It uses the same adapter and the same clips for every arm. It includes a causal-target arm (`ar_hcA` = `ar_z`, difference −0.000 [−0.006, +0.005]) and a format control (`ar_zfull`/`ar_hA`), and it rules out the linear adapter as the cause.
- **Genuinely informative constructive comparison.** The one-step transition from real observations is the same across all four predictors (0.355–0.388, TRAINED_PREDICTORS §3). The predictors differ only in multi-step evolution from a single boundary. That locates the gap cleanly, and it deserves more prominence than it gets.
- **Strong evaluation-audit results.**
  - Target leakage: pre-event tubelets are discriminated at 90.4% with the standard target and exactly 50.0% with the causal target.
  - Copy baseline: 85.00 against 88.89 on IntPhys 1.
  - On SSv2, blurred copies reproduce 61–79% of the predictor's advantage.
- **Honest scope.** Acceleration transfer is explicitly disclaimed.

## Weaknesses (ranked; minimal fix in brackets)

**W1. Most causal claims have no positive control, although one is available for free.** M5 (steering), M6 (history shift), M4 (knockout) and P3b (last token) were run only on the released predictor. As a result, "non-accumulation", "backward pull" and "the step is set by the last token" cannot be separated from intervention artifacts.
- The clearest case is §6.4: steering Δx at slot 0 is +0.12 cells, then falls to +0.04 at t7. It does not even accumulate over the ~3 steps that the draft's own few-step account predicts (§5.1).
- Either the ridge direction is not the velocity code the model uses, or the few-step extrapolation is not driven by it.
- [Run M5, M6, P3b and M4 unchanged on Ariel and pv1. If steering accumulates in Ariel, the released predictor's failure to accumulate means something. If it does not, withdraw §6.4.]

**W2. The steering intervention (M5, §6.4) is not specific.**
- It perturbs only 18 object tokens in the last two tubelets. M13 §3-2 shows velocity is decodable from windows with no object and from background tokens (t14: target background window 0.905, predictor output at the last-observed cell 0.92). The predictor therefore has redundant, now conflicting velocity sources.
- The random-direction control is weak: in 1280 dimensions it is nearly orthogonal to everything the model uses.
- The direction is not orthogonalised against position.
- [Steer all context tokens, or all tokens of the last two tubelets. Orthogonalise the direction against a position ridge direction. Add a nuisance-attribute direction of matched norm as the control, and measure displacement with soft-argmax.]

**W3. The knockout (M4, §6.2) cannot claim necessity without a specificity control.**

**W4. The slope metric in §5.3 cannot distinguish "reads a step" from "copies the fed position".**

- The raw data in `/data/hyuntak/project/2026/2027_cvpr/vjepa2/auto_research/exp_results/p3/p3_v3_p3b.json` settle this for the truth-cell template: `tf_first` lands on the new cell 0.84–0.95 and on the fed cell ≤ 0.14.

**W5. The "step" may be a window-end feature of the causal encoder, not object state.**

- A true observation of the right position is therefore not enough. The token must be the last tubelet of a causal encoding window.

**W6. The appearance template does see the future.** The draft (§3, P3/M13 docs) says the appearance template "uses no future truth". # Review: "Seeing Is Not Evolving" (DRAFT v1, 2026-09-25). Statistics / experimental-design referee

## Summary
The paper studies the frozen released V-JEPA 2 ViT-H. It argues four things:
- **(i) Encoder.** The encoder exposes velocity and direction, and summarizes motion history into the boundary tokens.
- **(ii) Released predictor.** It extrapolates a few steps from the last token, is pulled back toward past positions, and stalls when fed its own outputs.
- **(iii) Trained predictors.** Future-trained predictors on the same frozen encoder carry the object much further, so the encoder is not the bottleneck.
- **(iv) VoE surprise.** Surprise does not track evolution ability.

Evidence comes from synthetic RollOut_v3 with parameter-free template readouts, positional nulls, copy and oracle baselines, and trajectory-cluster bootstraps. It is supplemented by v11 occlusion twins, IntPhys 1 and SSv2.

## Strengths
1. The instrumentation is well above the field norm:
   - positional "other-trajectory" nulls;
   - copy, constant-velocity, label-mean and freeze-K oracles;
   - paired within-clip contrasts;
   - trajectory-cluster bootstraps on v3;
   - templates with and without future truth.
2. **The P3 closure test is a strong design.** The same adapter A carries either causal observations or self-predictions. `ar_hcA − ar_z` is −0.000 [−0.006, +0.005] (confirmed). The bidirectional-format control (`ar_h`, 0.212) turned a naive "content, not interface" reading into an honest decomposition: format −0.148, content −0.224.
3. The audit trail is exemplary. Retractions are kept in place, pre-registered plans exist (`PLAN_2026-09-24`, `PLAN_ENCODER_PREDICTOR_2026-09-25`), and an adversarial verifier re-derived every headline number with independent code.
4. The evaluation-audit results are genuinely new for machine VoE:
   - the IntPhys 1 copy baseline (85.00 vs 88.89);
   - bidirectional-target leakage (pre-event tubelets 90.4%);
   - the protocol sensitivity of p − copy (+3.9 / +12.2 / −3.9 across cells).

## Weaknesses (ranked; each with the minimal fix)

**W1. The abstract and contributions outrun the verified evidence, and the [V] tag hides the grade.**
- [V] means "numbers reproduced", not "claim confirmed". Several [V] items were graded *suggestive* or even *retracted* in `VERIFY_0925`:
  - §4.3 "Sufficiency" is tagged [V], but VERIFY graded it suggestive. It is also the framing M13 explicitly retracted: "encoder is enough / using only encoder-read state".
  - §5.2 SSv2 diagonal is suggestive.
  - §5.1 integration gain 0.4–0.7 is suggestive.
  - M4 overall is suggestive.
- Three of the four abstract pillars rest on first-pass [1st] work:
  - (ii) "step set by the last token alone" (P3b) and "pulled back" (M6);
  - (iii) all of §7;
  - (iv) "non-monotone" (n = 4).
- **Minimal fix:** give every claim a grade (confirmed / suggestive / first-pass). Allow only confirmed claims in the abstract and the page-1 list, or finish verification round 2 before submission.

**W2. The constructive localization (§7) is reported only at stride 1, and it is time-scale confounded.**
- Pretraining uses 4 fps (0.5 s tubelets). v3 at stride 1 is 0.067 s per tubelet, 7.5× faster. Ariel was trained at 12 fps, and pv1 / v11_postft at raw stride 3.
- `exp_results/cmp/cmp_predictors.json` already contains the stride-4 arm (7.5 fps). There the ordering on position disappears or reverses:

| s4_C8 | released | pv1 | Ariel | v11_postft |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| hit−null t2 | 0.281 | 0.171 | 0.285 | 0.145 |
| hit−null t3 | 0.139 | 0.018 | 0.174 | −0.031 |
| cliff slot | 2.46 | 1.93 | 2.02 | 1.70 |

- Objectness at t3 goes the other way: 0.013 (released) vs 0.753 (Ariel). So which readout "wins" also flips with stride.
- The remaining statistical problems in §7:
  - The table has **no paired CIs for between-predictor differences**. "Ariel best" rests on +0.24 vs +0.23 and 0.73 vs 0.67.
  - The row "hit vs extrapolator 0.74" compares raw hits whose nulls differ by 2.5×: 0.20 / 0.47 / 0.50 / 0.24 for the predictors, 0.51 for z_cv.
  - z_cv uses ground-truth pixel positions and is scored by true px cell, not by template argmax. So it is neither a ceiling nor the same readout.
  - The appearance-template row has no null at all; TRAINED_PREDICTORS §3-1 says so.
- **Minimal fix:**
  - Report §7 at s1, s2 and s4, including the full-visibility s4 subset (252 clips).
  - Use hit−null throughout, with paired trajectory-bootstrap CIs of differences.
  - Add nulls to the appearance template.
  - Restate the claim as: "at 30 fps, predictors trained at faster time scales place objects further; at 7.5 fps the released predictor's placement matches Ariel."

**W3. Several headline claims flip with the readout, and no primary readout was pre-specified.**

| Claim | Readout where it holds | Readout where it fails or flips |
|---|---|---|
| P3b "last token alone" | True-cell template: tf_first +0.023 vs tf_last −0.102 | Appearance template: tf_first −0.067 [−0.106, −0.032] ≈ tf_last −0.071 [−0.102, −0.036]. The asymmetry vanishes |

| Ariel "carries further" | Templates | TrainingEffects attn-ruler reach p@p 65 px < released 82 px; objectness t10 0.49 |

- The two templates are biased in opposite directions: true-cell toward truth, appearance toward the last-seen cell (0.36 pull at j0). Only claims that hold under both are robust.
- **Minimal fix:** declare one primary readout per claim. Add a readout-robustness table (both templates, objectness-gated, soft-argmax displacement). Demote claims whose sign or CI changes across readouts.

**W4. Pre-registered decision rules were not followed or not reported first.**
- **P2** (pre-registered: integer t\* and a clip regression):
  - Result: t\* = 8 / 5 / 3 tubelets, t\*·2s = 16 / 20 / 24 raw frames, so neither a tubelet limit nor a physical limit.

  - By the rule's own second branch, this reads "history is used; placement fails", which is not the §6.1 headline.

**W5. Forking paths and dependent "convergence".**
- About 20 analyses and about 30 retractions or narrowings in roughly 48 h. The story was re-fit after each one:
  - "radius" became "few steps";
  - "history unused" became "history pulls back";
  - "encoder is enough" was retracted, then reinstated via §7.

**W7. §8 "non-monotone" is not a statistical claim.**
- With n = 4 predictors, the ranks are actually *decreasing*: released has the lowest evolution and highest IntPhys; Ariel the reverse. That is not "non-monotone".
- Ariel's IntPhys score is confounded:
  - it was trained on ≤ 8 prediction slots, but the chosen cell skip2_w32 asks for 14;
  - it was trained at 12 fps with block-causal attention;
  - each predictor receives its own grid-max cell, which is a selection effect.
- **Minimal fix:** use one pre-specified cell for all predictors, plus the causal target. Or downgrade the claim to "no positive association among 4 predictors".

**W8. The P3b attribution is confounded.**
- The "true last token" z\*_{j−1} is produced by the context encoder from *all* true frames, so it carries encoder-integrated history. P3b cannot separate "the last token" from "history summarized by the encoder into the last token", which is the draft's own §6 synthesis.
- "Harmless history" is an equivalence claim. It needs a margin, especially since tf_first − ar_z = +0.023 has a CI that excludes 0.
- **Minimal fix:** rephrase as "predictor-level history tokens matter less than the latest encoder token", and use TOST with a declared margin.

**W9. Clarity invites a misreading.**

- **Minimal fix:** put the four-row table (one-shot, self-chain, observation-chain probe, trained predictor one-shot) on page 2, as in P3 §0.

**W10. The hedging alternative is only in the limitations, even though the paper holds a partial test.**
- The trained predictors share the deterministic L1 loss and the same targets but lag less.
- **Minimal fix:** use that comparison explicitly to argue that the lag is not intrinsic to L1 hedging, with the stride caveat from W2.

# Review: "Seeing Is Not Evolving" (DRAFT_v1, 2026-09-25)
**Reviewer persona:** robotics / embodied world models / video prediction

## Summary
The paper studies the frozen V-JEPA 2 ViT-H encoder with its released predictor on synthetic motion (RollOut_v3, IntPhysGen v11), IntPhys 1 and 677 SSv2 left/right clips. It uses parameter-free template readouts, positional nulls, copy, constant-velocity and freeze-K baselines, closure (feedback) tests, and patching and knockout interventions. It makes four claims:
- (i) Velocity is linearly readable from the encoder tokens the predictor consumes.
- (ii) The released predictor extrapolates a few steps, lags, and fades. Fed its own outputs, it stalls.
- (iii) Three already-trained future predictors on the same frozen encoder carry the object much further, so "the encoder is not the bottleneck".
- (iv) VoE surprise accuracy does not track this ability.

## Strengths
1. **Measurement discipline beyond what most JEPA-analysis papers do.** Parameter-free readouts, same-law other-trajectory nulls, trajectory-cluster CIs (84 trajectories × 28 appearances), copy and oracle baselines, and pre-registered decision rules. Corrections are kept visible in the evidence docs.
2. **The evaluation-audit results are useful to anyone who uses V-JEPA surprise as a metric or a reward.**
   - Bidirectional-target leakage: pixel-identical pre-event tubelets are discriminated at 90.4% with the standard target. A causal target gives exactly 50.0% (all ties).
   - Copy-of-last-latent reaches 85.00 on IntPhys 1 at the best grid cell.
   - On SSv2, blurred copies reproduce 61–79% of the L1 advantage. Any practitioner using V-JEPA surprise as a reward (WMReward-style) should know this.
3. **The closure test is well designed.** The same adapter A carries causal observations (slope 0.361, identical to `ar_z`) and self-predictions (−0.012). This rules out the linear-interface explanation. The form effect (−0.148) is separated from the content effect (−0.224).
4. **The constructive "swap the predictor, keep the encoder" design is the right experiment.** It needs no training and can answer the question either way.

## Weaknesses (ranked, each with the minimal fix)

**W1. §7 is reported at one frame rate, the advantage disappears at others, and the section is unverified ([1st]).**
The same file behind §7 (`exp_results/cmp/cmp_predictors.json`) contains stride 2 and stride 4 arms. Cliff slot (released / pv1 / Ariel / v11_postft):

| stride | released | pv1 | Ariel | v11_postft |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| s1 | 6.57 | 11.42 | 11.75 | 2.55 |
| s2 | 4.11 | 5.54 | 4.92 | 3.90 |
| s4 | 2.46 | 1.93 | 2.02 | 1.70 |

- Last-slot hit−null at s4 is .139 / .018 / .174 / −.031.
- In seconds, the released position horizon is about 0.44 / 0.55 / 0.66 s across strides. Ariel's is about 0.78 / 0.66 / 0.54 s.
- So "far beyond" means roughly +0.3 s, at 30 fps only. That is 7.5× the 4 fps pretraining rate. At s4 (4 context → 4 predicted slots, 7.5 fps) Ariel is in its own training regime in both slot count and time, yet shows no position advantage.
- At s4 the trained predictors keep objectness (Ariel t3 0.75 vs released 0.01) but place the object no better. This presence/position split should be reported.
- **Fix:** report the full predictor × stride table in seconds with trajectory CIs. Add stride 3, the training stride of pv1 and v11. Re-run s4 with entry-aligned windows (P2 correction 7: the object is visible in a median of 2 of 4 context tubelets). Verify adversarially. Scope the claim to fine temporal resolution.

**W2. "Encoder is enough / encoder-state extrapolator" overclaims.**
- The 0.74 extrapolator uses the **true** last pixel position, a supervised ridge velocity, and a hand-coded constant-velocity rule. M13 §4 itself says this panel does not support "the encoder is sufficient".
- A label-mean velocity already reaches 0.56–0.61. The z-specific gain is +0.14 to +0.19.
- On arc, p beats the extrapolator: .85 vs .50 at t6, .55 vs .00 at t8.
- Acceleration is not readable at C16 (H1: r8 R² 0.51 [0.25, 0.65]; C8 0.01), and no predictor transfers it (TrainingEffects §2-2).
- The appearance-template ceiling is 0.80 at about 0.67 s.
- Without encoder retraining (excluded by the constraints), the paper cannot tell practitioners "retrain the predictor, not the encoder". It can say that a predictor swap reaches the encoder's readout ceiling for first-order extrapolation up to about 0.7 s in a single-object synthetic scene. JEPA-WMs, where encoder choice matters for planning, should be discussed as the counterpoint.
- **Fix:** rename the §7 row "oracle-position constant-velocity extrapolator". Narrow the title and abstract (iii). Mention the arc reversal in the text.

**W3. Page-1 finding (b) does not survive the paper's own control.**
- `tf_first` (self-predicted history, true last token) is +0.023 with the truth-cell template.
- With the appearance template it is **−0.067 [−0.106, −0.032]**, which cannot be told apart from `tf_last` (−0.071) (P3B §3).
- §3 says truth-cell templates can be pulled toward the true cell by background, and the appearance template is the paper's no-future-truth control.
- **Fix:** report both templates. Keep only what holds under both: a self-predicted last token slows the step, and a self-predicted chain is far worse (−0.373 / −0.314). Keep the claim "history is harmless" out of the page-1 findings.

**W4. §5.1 misstates the freeze-K oracle.**
- R² 0.99 is how well the descriptive D0 + kδ form fits the oracle's *own* cliffs (1.51 + 2.70δ). It is not a fit of p's curve (p: 2.62 + 1.96δ, R² 0.944).
- P2 §3-5 shows p follows the truth for 5 tubelets at s1, 3–5 at s2 and 3 at s4. There is no single K.
- **Fix:** reword §5.1 and the v0→v1 changelog line.

**W5. The VoE non-monotonicity claim (§8, abstract iv) is confounded.**
- There are only 4 predictors, and data, architecture, initialization, fps and epoch all differ at once.
- Ariel's IntPhys 1 best cell (skip2_w32, C = 4) queries 14 future slots, but Ariel was trained for at most 8 (TrainingEffects §1-1). It was also trained on 48-frame windows at 12 fps.
- Ariel − copy is −16.1 with the standard target but −7.8 with the causal target.
- The abstract's "copying nearly matches" is a best-cell statement. At the official skip2_w16 cell, p − copy is +12.2 [+6.7, +17.8].
- **Fix:** see E2 below. Make the abstract two-sided.

**W6. Stale or inconsistent numbers.**
- **§6.1:** "recovery 0.94" and "static destruction 0.36" come from the withdrawn 0.234 threshold, whose pool included empty scenes.
  - Corrected M2: recovery for moving objects is 1.10 (continuous) / 1.12 at the 0.313 threshold.
  - Static destruction is 0.53–0.71.
  - Position recovery for moving objects is 0.15 (constant velocity 0.03).
  - The presence readout also fires on empty-scene background templates (0.53–0.59), so it measures template reproduction.
- **§7:** raw hits (null 0.20 released vs 0.47–0.50 trained) are mixed with hit−null rows. Report hit−null throughout.
- **§6.3:** the base_iso − base effect is −0.56 at t4, larger than any of the time-shift effects, and it is not reported. At t4 the dose-response is non-monotone (hist−8 +0.02 vs hist−4 −0.18). hist+4 includes the boundary frames themselves.
- **§4.2:** bndM uses a temporal-prefix mask. That is out of distribution for an encoder pretrained with tube masks that span the whole clip in time, so "history summarized into boundary tokens" is confounded with encoder distribution shift.

**W7. The importance and use case are thin for an embodied audience.**
- The cited uses are VoE evaluation, EK100 (where the predictor adds +0.6 per V-JEPA 2 Table 20), surprise rewards and HERA. Planning is explicitly excluded.
- V-JEPA 2-AC, DINO-WM and DINO-world already freeze the encoder and train a future predictor. DINO-world also reports collapse near 1 s.
- §9's requirements (multi-step future-only training with self-feedback, causal targets) repeat known practice: PlaNet overshooting, 2-AC rollout T = 2, Self/Diffusion Forcing, pushforward.
- The most robotics-relevant result is not framed as such. With fresh observations the released predictor advances the object every step at 82% of the true slope (0.361 / 0.44). That is the closed-loop MPC regime, and it is good news.
- **Fix:** organize the paper around closed-loop (re-observed) versus open-loop (blind) use. Report horizons in seconds next to typical control rates. State which result changes a practitioner's decision.

**W8. Representativeness of the testbed.**
- **RollOut_v3:** a single rigid object, static camera, mostly horizontal motion, no contact (the ledge and wall arms are outside the main claims), 30 fps, 56 held-out trajectories in P3, and an 18 px readout grid.
- **SSv2:**
  - It is inside the pretraining data, and the direction task is 1 bit.
  - Distances are pooled over whole scenes and include camera and hand motion.
  - No trained predictor is compared.
  - There is no hedging ceiling. The "error-matched progressing oracle" contains the true progression by construction, so it cannot separate missing progression from correct hedging under an uncertain future. The 15% magnitude ratio is exactly what an L1 conditional-median predictor would produce.

**W9. Presentation invites the misreading answered above.**
- Findings (a) and (b) read as if autoregressive rollout works.
- **Fix:** put P3 §0's four-row table in §5.3 (one-shot / self-feed / real-observation feed / window-encoded tokens). State plainly that `ar_z` uses future frames.
- More broadly, most of the novel sections are [1st] and unverified: §4.2, token-level §5.2, §5.3, §6.3, §6.4, §7, and §8's non-monotonicity.
