## Abstract

The pretrained V-JEPA 2 predictor is used as a world model: intuitive-physics benchmarks score it by violation-of-expectation (VoE) surprise, and downstream work uses it for forecasting and rewards.
It was pretrained to fill space-time tube masks, not to predict the future. We ask what it actually does when observations stop, and whether VoE accuracy can tell.

We hold the released encoder and target encoder fixed and compare four predictors that already exist: the released one, two fine-tuned future-only, and one trained from scratch future-only on natural video. We test them on controlled synthetic motion and real video, using parameter-free readouts with positional nulls, copy and constant-velocity oracles, and trajectory-level statistics. Three findings:

(i) **In a single query, the released predictor moves visible objects at about the true speed for roughly three tubelets, then freezes them at a velocity-proportional position.** Object presence fades with time since the last observation. Fed its own outputs one tubelet at a time, it stops after two steps. Real observations keep it moving, which shows that a one-step transition exists.

(ii) **Predictors trained for future-only queries on the same frozen encoder follow the true path for about eight tubelets, roughly twice as far, before they too stall near their own training horizon.** One of them was trained only on natural video and is tested on unseen synthetic renders. The released predictor's limit is therefore not set by the encoder input alone.

(iii) **VoE accuracy does not certify this ability.**
- At the best IntPhys 1 cell, copying the last observed latent is statistically indistinguishable from the released predictor.
- A bidirectional target encoder lets pixel-identical pre-event frames be discriminated (90.4 %, vs. 50.0 % with a causal target).
- On IntPhys 1, surprise accuracy ranks the four predictors differently from their extrapolation.

We release the diagnostic suite (closure tests, twin patching, stride sweeps, copy / oracle baselines) as a protocol for evaluating latent world models by what they evolve, not by how surprised they are.

## 1. Introduction

**P1 — What a latent world model must do.** A latent world model maintains a belief state sufficient for predicting the latent future. It advances that state with a transition that stays predictive when no new observation arrives [POMDP/PSR; PlaNet; LeCun 2022].
Generative video models were recently shown to freeze or reset unobserved states [StEvo-Bench; WRBench]. Latent predictive models split the work explicitly: the encoder reads, the predictor advances. That split lets us ask where the advance happens.

**P2 — Why this predictor, and the out-of-regime caveat up front.** The pretrained V-JEPA 2 predictor is the module credited with world knowledge:
- VoE surprise on IntPhys and IntPhys 2 [Garrido et al.; Bordes et al.];
- EK100 anticipation features [V-JEPA 2];
- surprise rewards [WMReward];
- memory adapters [HERA].

It was pretrained with tube masks spanning the whole clip (`temporal_scale [1,1]`) at 4 fps. Future-only queries are therefore off-label, and our 30 fps synthetic clips are 7.5× finer than its training rate.
We treat this as an evaluation-validity question: the evaluations use the module off-label and credit it with physics. We report every horizon in tubelets and seconds, and we test the stride. (Planning in V-JEPA 2-AC trains a new predictor; we make no planning claim.)

**P3 — Four ways to produce a future (page-1 table).** Readers conflate these; our results depend on the distinction.

| mode | appended each step | uses future frames? | released, v3, slope over j3–7 (truth 0.44) |
|---|---|---|---|
| one-shot query (standard use) | nothing | no | ≈ 0 after ~3 tubelets (freezes) |
| chain own predictions, one tubelet at a time | its own prediction | no | −0.01 [−0.04, +0.02] — stops after two steps |
| append real observations (probe) | encoded real next frames | **yes** | 0.36 — one-step transition exists |
| a different, forecasting-trained predictor, one shot | nothing | no | follows ≈ 8 tubelets, then stalls (§5) |

**P4 — Contributions.**
1. **A VoE audit (§4).**
   - Copy baselines for the latent-surprise protocol.
   - Target leakage.
   - The failure of surprise accuracy to rank predictors by extrapolation.
2. **A constructive swap (§5).** With the encoder frozen, forecasting-trained predictors extend visible-object placement about 2×. This bounds how much of the released predictor's short horizon can be attributed to the encoder.
3. **A behavioural characterization of the released predictor (§6)**, with one causal result: future object content needs direct retrieval from context object tokens at 2–3-cell displacement.
4. **A diagnostic protocol.**

## 3. Setup

- **Model.** Released V-JEPA 2 ViT-H (fpc64, 256 px; pretrained at 4 fps, one tubelet = 0.5 s). The context encoder is the online encoder; the target is the EMA encoder with affine-free LN. Numerics are fp32 with fp16 autocast.
- **Predictors** (existing checkpoints, no new training):
  - released;
  - `predictor_v1_postft`: released, fine-tuned future-only on synthetic motion close to v3 (raw stride 3);
  - `v11_postft`: fine-tuned future-only on IntPhysGen v11;
  - Ariel: scratch, block-causal, future-only on SSv2 + K400 at 12 fps, contexts {2,4,8,16} × targets {1,2,4,8} tubelets.
- **Data.**
  - RollOut_v3: 30 fps, 64 frames. Free-motion clips are 84 trajectories × 28 appearances. Strides 1/2/4 give 67 / 133 / 267 ms per tubelet.
  - IntPhysGen v11: timing twins and visible twins.
  - IntPhys 1 dev (Garrido A.8 grid).
  - SSv2 left/right: 677 clips, ~5–12 fps.
- **Readouts.**
  - True-cell template argmax, reported as hit − null, where the null is every other trajectory of the same law. Primary for position.
  - Appearance template: the last-context object token. It does not use the true future location, but it is a bidirectional-target token.
  - Objectness under a common threshold, graded **S**. It does not transfer across predictors, whose contrast statistics differ.
- **Statistics.** Trajectory-cluster bootstrap (v3), block-cluster bootstrap (v11), clip bootstrap (SSv2). Pre-registered rules and outcomes are in the appendix, reported first.

## 4. The VoE audit **[C unless noted]**

- **4.1 Copy baselines.**
  - IntPhys 1, best grid cell (skip2_w32): copy 85.00 vs released 88.89, +3.9 [−0.6, +8.9].
  - The official cell skip2_w16 shows +9.4 to +12.2. Two copy implementations disagree by 4 pairs here, to be reconciled (B9).
  - On v11 (split test), the released margin over copy (+2.5) comes entirely from color violations; without color it is −1.7 [−2.6, −0.7] (from the parallel TrainingEffects analysis).
- **4.2 Target leakage.** Pixel-identical pre-event tubelets are discriminated at 90.4 % with the standard bidirectional target and 50.0 % with a causal target.
- **4.3 L1 rewards blur.** On SSv2, blurred copies reproduce 61–79 % of the predictor's L1 advantage over copy.
- **4.4 Ranking** **[S]**.
  - On IntPhys 1, the four predictors rank 88.89 (released) > 80.56 (pv1) > 77.22 (v11) > 69.44 (Ariel).
  - Their v3 extrapolation (§5) ranks Ariel ≳ pv1 > v11 > released.
  - On v11 (split test), the released predictor is lowest in both.
  - Confounds: Ariel's best IntPhys cell queries 14 slots beyond its 8-slot training horizon; fps and target differ. Within-run epochs (B1) will show whether the two co-vary.

## 5. The constructive swap: same frozen encoder, predictors trained for future-only queries **[C, scope: visible, near-constant-velocity motion, stride 1–2, no parabolas]**

v3, stride 1, C16/P32, null-corrected, trajectory CIs:

| | released | pv1 | Ariel | v11_postft |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| path slope over t3–7 / t8–11 / t12–15 (truth 0.46 / 0.45 / 0.44) | 0.07 / 0.03 / 0.03 | 0.52 / 0.24 / 0.00 | 0.48 / 0.29 / −0.08 | lead of 2–3 cells, then a stall |
| t10 hit − null | −0.14 [−0.18, −0.09] | +0.24 [0.17, 0.31] | +0.26 [0.21, 0.31] | +0.07 |
| t10 appearance hit − null (ceiling +0.32; constant-velocity oracle +0.31) | −0.14 | +0.23 | +0.26 | +0.08 |
| real-observation feedback slope | 0.36 | 0.37 | 0.39 | 0.36 |
| own-prediction chain slope | −0.01 | 0.14 | 0.23 | 0.15 |

- **Reading.**
  - All four predictors share the observation-fed one-step transition (0.36–0.39). They differ in open-loop evolution.
  - Forecasting-trained predictors follow the path about 2× as long (~8 tubelets), then stall at about their training horizon.
  - Ariel is the only one at or above the released predictor in all four windows. pv1 is worse with 4-tubelet contexts.
  - With stride 4 (7.5 fps, the stride nearest pretraining; context objects visible in a median of 2 of 4 tubelets) position advantages shrink (B6 pending).
- **Seconds.** At stride 1 the released predictor tracks ~0.2 s. The trained ones track ~0.5 s. At stride 2 they track ~0.4 s and ~1 s.

## 6. What the released predictor does **[C behaviour; S mechanism]**

- **6.1 Few-step extrapolation, then freezing** **[C]**. In a single query, the object moves at about true speed for ~3 tubelets and then freezes at a velocity-proportional position (stall slope b = 4.60 [4.01, 5.14] vs. truth 6.07).
  - The stride sweep rejects a fixed tubelet budget **[C]**. A freeze-after-3-steps oracle reproduces the position curve (R² 0.99) **[S]**.
  - Presence fades with time since the last observation **[S]**.
  - On SSv2, predicted slots track the true time index for ~3 slots, then flatten **[S]**.
- **6.2 Self-feedback.**
  - The chain stalls after two steps **[C]**.
  - Making either the fed history or the last fed token a real observation recovers ~78 % (appearance readout) **[S]**.
  - Tokens encoded with the whole window slow the step even when true (truth-cell readout only) **[S]**.
- **6.3 Presence is set at the boundary; placement needs more** (v11 twins, including visible twins) **[C for presence, S otherwise]**.
  - Swapping the last 1–2 context tubelets transfers presence between late-occluded, early-occluded and never-occluded twins: continuous-contrast recovery 1.10.
  - Placement recovers much less (0.15).
  - Boundary tokens encoded without seeing earlier frames still carry presence, but lose placement (hit − null −0.64). Whether this reflects multi-frame integration by the encoder is **H**.
- **6.4 Retrieval** **[S]**. Future object tokens attend selectively to context object tokens: 3–13× uniform, against 0.4–1.1× for same-distance non-object keys. Blocking that retrieval collapses hits at 2–3-cell displacements (necessity only).
- **6.5 History** **[C, scope]**. With natural history, the predictor's history tokens barely change placement (|Δ| ≤ 0.02) and contribute to presence.
- **6.6 Occlusion is a corner case.** Far slots are lost for never-occluded twins too.

## 7. Toward predictors that evolve state

Each requirement is tied to the evidence behind it:

| requirement | why | evidence |
|---|---|---|
| train the predictor for open-loop future queries beyond the target horizon | horizons track the training horizon | §5 (release ~3, trained ~8 tubelets, then stall) |
| closed-loop consistency (consume own outputs) | chains stall; training helps partially (0.14–0.23 of 0.44) | §5, §6.2 |
| a carried state rather than one-shot retrieval from context tokens | placement relies on context-token retrieval (§6.4); presence decays with time | §6.1, §6.4 |
| causal, time-consistent targets and inputs | leakage; window-encoded tokens | §4.2, §6.2 |
| evaluate by horizon curves, copy/oracle baselines and closure tests, not surprise accuracy | §4 | §4 |

## 8. Limitations

- One encoder; replication across scales and V-JEPA 2.1 is on hold.
- Synthetic-heavy; the SSv2 anchor is inside the pretraining distribution.
- Kinematics only; acceleration is neither read nor transferred.
- Strides nearest the pretraining rate are only partially testable (v3 clips are 2.1 s).
- The trained-predictor comparison confounds data, architecture, initialization and training stride.
- Readout dependence is reported per claim. Deterministic L1 predictors hedge; the objective is discussed in one paragraph, not blamed.
