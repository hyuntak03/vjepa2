**Seeing Is Not Evolving: The Pretrained V-JEPA 2 Predictor Updates on Observations but Does Not Carry the World State**

## Abstract (draft)

Latent video world models split the job in two: an encoder that represents what is observed, and a predictor that advances that representation into the future.
Intuitive-physics benchmarks and several downstream uses credit the *pretrained* V-JEPA 2 predictor with this second role.
We ask whether it actually evolves the latent state when new observations stop arriving.
On controlled synthetic scenes and real videos, the frozen encoder exposes the relevant state — object identity, position, and motion direction are linearly readable, and the target space still contains the object at distant future times — yet the predictor does not carry that state forward.
Its predictions are anchored to the last observed tokens:
(i) whether an object exists in the predicted future is decided by the last one or two context tubelets, while earlier context is barely used;
(ii) predicted objects survive only within a fixed spatial radius (~2.6 cells) of the last observation plus about two steps of motion, regardless of frame rate;
(iii) fed back its own predictions, the predictor stops advancing after about three steps, while real observations passed through the same interface keep it advancing.
Consequently, violation-of-expectation surprise does not certify state evolution: copying the last observed latent approaches the predictor's accuracy on IntPhys, and part of the signal leaks from a bidirectional target encoder.
We distill these diagnostics into design requirements and a signature-based test suite for state-evolving predictors.

## 1. Introduction

**P1 — world model = read + evolve.** A latent world model maintains a belief state sufficient for predicting the latent future, and advances that state with a transition that stays predictive when no new observation arrives [POMDP/PSR, PlaNet, LeCun 2022]. Generative video world models were recently shown to freeze or reset states that leave the view [StEvo-Bench, WRBench]. We ask the same question of a *latent predictive* model, where the division of labor is explicit: the encoder reads, the predictor evolves.

**P2 — why the pretrained predictor.** The pretrained V-JEPA 2 predictor is used as a world model in practice: VoE surprise on IntPhys [Garrido et al. 2025; IntPhys 2], forecasting features for EK100 anticipation [V-JEPA 2], surprise-based rewards [WMReward], and memory adapters built on top of it [HERA]. It was pretrained with tube masks spanning the whole clip (`temporal_scale [1,1]`), so future-only queries are outside its training distribution — we treat this as an *evaluation-validity* question: the evaluations attribute world knowledge to a module used out of its training regime. (Planning in V-JEPA 2-AC trains a new predictor; we make no planning claim.)

**P3 — the tension.** IntPhys 1 is solved (88.89 pairwise), IntPhys 2 is near chance; IntPhys 2 reviewers asked "is it memory, or representation and perception?" [IntPhys 2 OpenReview]. We answer with a component-level localization.

**P4 — contributions.**
1. *Encoder sufficiency at the predictor interface*: state variables are readable from the final encoder tokens the predictor consumes (synthetic + SSv2), and the target space retains the object at far horizons.
2. *A causal localization of the bottleneck in the predictor*: boundary patching with timing twins, a closure test, a stride sweep that identifies the unit of the horizon.
3. *An evaluation audit*: copy-last-latent and causal-target controls for VoE surprise.
4. *Design requirements + a signature test suite* for predictors that must evolve state.

**Non-obvious findings (page 1).** (a) One-step transitions exist — feeding real observations advances the object every step. (b) The failure is in the predicted tokens, not the interface — the same adapter carries real observations fine. (c) The horizon is a spatial radius, not a step budget — tripling the stride shifts it in steps, not in cells.

## 3. Setup

- Model: released V-JEPA 2 ViT-H (fpc64, 256px); context encoder = online encoder, target = EMA encoder with affine-free LN; predictor 12 blocks, mask token [0]; fp32 + fp16 autocast (the official scoring numerics).
- Data:
  - **RollOut_v3** — 3,136 clips, 64 frames at 30 fps, 8 motion laws; free-motion subset 2,352 = 84 trajectories × 28 appearances (all CIs are trajectory-cluster bootstraps).
  - **IntPhysGen v11** — 43,008 clips in pixel-matched 4-tuples; occlusion timing twins (early / late).
  - **IntPhys 1 dev** (Garrido protocol).
  - **SSv2** left/right motion subset (722 clips; 698 with ≥32 frames).
- Readouts (validated):
  - Parameter-free template readout: cosine argmax against the true-cell token of the standard target, with a positional null (another trajectory of the same law) and a common objectness threshold.
  - Appearance-template readout (last seen object token; no future truth).
  - Linear probes with trajectory/clip splits.
- Controls: encoder-side ceilings (h, zf), copy-last-latent (standard and causal), constant-velocity oracle, positional null.

## 4. The encoder is enough (at the interface the predictor consumes)

- 4.1 Read. SSv2 direction from context-encoder tokens per tubelet: 0.89–0.97 (forward / reversed, trained with reversal-flipped labels so scene cues cannot solve it) **[1st]**. Synthetic: velocity from z — M3 **[pending]**; identity (v11): color/env 97–100 **[conf, prior]**.
- 4.2 The target space keeps the future. The appearance template of the last seen object finds the object in the true future target tokens at every horizon (0.75–0.91 across strides), while on the predictor output it drops to 0–0.05 after the cliff **[1st, P2]**.
- 4.3 Observations suffice to advance. Fresh encoder observations fed back advance the object every step (new-cell landing 0.81–0.95; slope 0.361 vs truth 0.440) **[conf H5, 1st P3]**.
- 4.4 Sufficiency extrapolator (0-parameter): constant-velocity extrapolation from encoder-read velocity vs predictor far-slot hits **[pending M3]**.
- Caveat box: acceleration is not reliably readable at short context (H1); encoder-side probing itself is not our contribution (Joseph et al. 2026).

## 5. The predictor does not carry the state

- 5.1 Behaviour. One-shot far slots lose the object; position lags constant-velocity extrapolation **[conf H23/H4]**. On SSv2 the predicted slots do not progress in time: nearest real time index j* ≈ 8 for all eight future slots (slope 0.01), while the encoder control is exactly diagonal (slope 1.00) **[1st]**.
- 5.2 Unit of the horizon (P2). Stride 1/2/4 × 3 speed terciles: cliff distance = 2.64 cells + 1.89 × per-tubelet displacement (R² 0.91; step-, time-, distance-only models R² ≤ 0; leave-one-stride-out error 0.13–0.42 cells) **[1st]**.
- 5.3 No inference-time use extends it: single-query, layer lens, attention knockout, self-feedback **[conf H4/H23/H8/H5]**.

## 6. Where exactly: causal localization

- 6.1 Boundary patching (M2): swapping only the last 1–2 context tubelets between timing twins transfers the objectness gap in both directions (t2–4 recovery 0.94, destruction 1.22); deleting all earlier context leaves the early twin unchanged (+0.00) **[1st]**.
- 6.2 Closure (P3): with the same adapter, causal target observations advance like encoder observations (slope 0.361 = 0.361), self-predictions stop at j≈3 (−0.012) → content, not interface **[1st]**.
- 6.3 Effective memory & velocity code (M1/M3) **[pending]**; attention radius by stride (M4) **[pending]**.
- Synthesis: an observation-anchored few-step extrapolator — reads motion from the last observed tokens, moves the object for ~2 steps within a ~2.6-cell radius, and has no carried state that its own outputs can continue from.

## 7. Consequences for evaluation

- IntPhys 1 best grid cell: copy-last-latent 85.00 vs predictor 88.89 (+3.9 [−0.6, +8.4], n.s.); the official cell skip2_w16 shows a significant +12.2 → protocol-sensitive, report the full grid **[conf]**.
- Bidirectional target leakage: pre-event tubelets discriminate 90.4% (standard) → 50.0% (causal) **[conf]**.
- L1 comparisons reward blur: on SSv2 the predictor beats the causal copy by ~28% at every horizon without progressing in time — blur baselines **[pending E-SSv2 v2]**.
- Recommended protocol: copy + causal-target controls, horizon curves in cells and seconds, closure test.

## 8. Toward state-evolving predictors (design requirements)

Derived from §5–6, not from layer localization. Each requirement comes with the signature it must remove:

| requirement | removes signature |
|---|---|
| carried recurrent state (state tokens updated each step) | M2 boundary determinism; far-slot decay |
| multi-step open-loop training with self-feedback | P3 self-feedback stall |
| observation-dropout / blackout training | late-occlusion loss (v11) |
| future-only, variable-Δt training; relative/absolute time positions | P2 radius-bound cliff; RoPE non-relativity |
| causal targets | leakage-driven scores |

Minimal test (proposed, needs a user decision on training): a small predictor on the frozen encoder, trained on synthetic motion, compared against copy, constant velocity, the released predictor, and an action-free 2-AC-style predictor, judged by the signatures above rather than by scores.

## 9. Limitations

Single model (ViT-H; robustness on ViT-L/g, V-JEPA 2.1 pending) · synthetic-heavy with an SSv2 anchor · kinematics only (no contact dynamics) · deterministic L1 predictor — hedging vs anchoring is argued behaviourally (discussion paragraph only for the objective).
